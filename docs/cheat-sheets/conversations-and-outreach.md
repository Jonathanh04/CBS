# Cheat sheet — Conversations & outreach rules

*One page. Questions to ask, objections to prepare for, and rules for outreach and data.*
Part of the [lean specification](../lean-spec.md).

## 1. Questions to ask (pick 3)

Their goals are something to **ask about**, not to assume.

- "Which profiles are hardest for you to reach right now?"
- "What would a successful student partnership look like for you?"
- "What has worked well, or not, in your other student partnerships?"
- "Who else would usually be involved in a decision like this?"
- "When do you plan next year's student and employer-branding activities?"
- "Is there a topic you'd like to be part of the conversation on?"

## 2. Objections to prepare for

These are **possibilities to prepare for, not predictions.** Never tell a company what you think their objection is.

| You may hear… | Prepare | Facts about CBS Talks you need |
|---|---|---|
| "We already work with other CBS organisations." | Ask what those deliver; offer something different (format, topic, audience), not more of the same | Audience mix, formats, past partners |
| "We have no budget for this." | Offer a non-cash first step; ask when budgets are planned; log a follow-up date | Non-cash options |
| "What do we get? How many people, which profiles?" | Show real attendance and study-line numbers; offer a partner report after the event | Event data, a sample partner report |
| "CBS students aren't our target." | Name the matching study lines, or switch to a speaker/topic angle, or accept it | Study-line mix |
| "Our executives don't have time." | Offer a 30-minute fireside chat, another expert, or a later date | Format options |
| "That's decided by our head office abroad." | Ask the local contact to champion it; offer a short English one-pager | English media kit |
| "We'd want a say in the content." | Explain the independence policy clearly: what they can shape (their own talk, a case) and what they can't | Independence policy |
| "Student organisations change every year." | Show the handover routine and a named continuity contact; put deliverables in writing | Handover routine, past renewals |

**If you don't have the fact, don't make it up.** Say you'll send it, then send it.

## 3. Outreach rules

- **One-to-one, personal messages only.** No mass mailings.
- **Say where you found them** ("I was introduced by…" / "I found you on LinkedIn") and make it easy to say no.
- **Verified facts only** in messages. Never state our inferences about them ("you're unknown among students").
- **No budget talk in a first cold message.** Don't mention what other companies pay.
- **Check before sending:** no other board member is already in contact; the contact is still in the role; the company isn't marked do-not-contact.
- After **3 unanswered attempts** over at least 3 weeks: stop, mark Lost ("No response") or Nurture.
- **To verify with CBS / legal before the first campaign:** how Danish rules on unsolicited electronic marketing apply to partnership requests from a student organisation.

## 4. Data rules (GDPR)

- Store **work-related data only:** name, title, company, published work email or LinkedIn, source, date.
- **No personal opinions about people** in notes or interactions.
- **No scraping and no guessed email addresses.**
- "Please don't contact me" → mark **do not contact** immediately.
- Delete contacts with no interaction for **24 months**, and contacts who left the company once handed over.
- AI-drafted information is **unchecked** until a person has opened the source. Label everything: *Verified fact / Source-backed inference / Unknown*.
